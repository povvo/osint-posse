# Imagery Art Direction

Output path: `foundations/imagery-art-direction.md`

## Image Role Taxonomy

- **Hero:** one iconic event in a portrait-first field; full atmospheric treatment is permitted.
- **Product:** use clean, accurate product imagery when inspection, colour, condition, fit, or safety matters. Confine the atmospheric language to the surrounding frame or campaign layer.
- **Editorial:** strongest fit. Use a solitary subject, relational cluster, or processional vista with suspended narrative.
- **Instructional:** diagrams and steps remain crisp, literal, and low-texture. Decorative imagery may introduce a section but cannot carry the instruction.
- **Avatar:** do not use anonymous silhouettes for real users, staff, testimonials, or identity. Use accessible portraits, initials, or user-selected images.
- **Empty state:** one compact silhouette or route cue, low emotional severity, explicit explanation, and a direct next action.
- **Texture:** seamless particulate field or localized chromatic edge material; never a substitute for content.
- **Background:** absorptive negative space with one restrained thermal field. It must not compromise text, focus, or controls.

## Subject Guidance

- **Who or what appears:** people, animals, vehicles, tools, architecture, landscape, atmosphere, or abstract masses may all belong. Choose subjects for the actual narrative, not to prove a frontier theme.
- **What must remain visible:** the category-defining contour, stance or direction, principal relationship, and one governing prop or environmental cue.
- **What must never be cropped away:** the only identifying bend, head profile, limb separation, animal hump/horn, wheel/route turn, or interaction point that makes the subject legible.
- **Proximity and viewpoint:** use full or three-quarter iconic views for solitary subjects, bracketed medium views for gatherings, and elevated or long views for processions. Avoid face-dominant beauty framing and extreme wide-angle distortion.
- **Anonymity boundary:** feature suppression can create archetype and tension in fictional/editorial scenes. It must not erase identity where recognition, representation, consent, accountability, or human dignity matters.
- **Narrative module rule:** clothing, headwear, weapons, shelters, vessels, flora, animals, and terrain are optional context modules. Research them for the intended culture and period; never use them as generic exotic decoration.

## Photography Direction

- **Lens feel:** compressed, poster-like, and observational—roughly a normal-to-short-telephoto feel for figures and a moderate wide view for landscapes. Keep verticals and subject proportions credible.
- **Lighting:** one warm practical or ground source plus one cool ambient/rim field, or the inverse. Preserve a dark structural core and allow selective edge bloom.
- **Depth of field:** moderate-to-deep for silhouette relationships; atmospheric haze may soften distance. Do not use shallow bokeh to replace composition.
- **Colour grade:** near-black shadows, selective orange-red/amber, muted cyan/teal, and scarce pale highlights. Grade locally by plane instead of applying a uniform orange/cyan preset.
- **Realism level:** stylized editorial realism. Physical light may be heightened, but anatomy, object function, and safety-relevant details remain credible.
- **Imperfections:** particulate texture, mild diffusion, and local chromatic spill are allowed. Avoid fake film scratches, heavy halation, crushed detail on subjects that require inspection, and copied compression defects.
- **Capture plan:** expose to protect the pale convergence zone, retain a clean high-dynamic-range master, and build grain/thermal separation non-destructively after colour correction.

## Illustration Direction

- **Shape language:** broad dark masses, simplified contour landmarks, and one thin directional cue. Build with value regions before colour and texture.
- **Line quality:** little or no conventional linework. Use lost-and-found edges, soft pigment dispersion, and crisp internal negative cuts where recognition requires them.
- **Detail density:** low. Allocate detail to one identifying contour and one focal prop or relationship; suppress facial, fabric, architectural, and terrain microdetail.
- **Texture:** multiscale granular stipple across values, with chromatic density increasing near illuminated transitions. Retain an achromatic structural core.
- **Character rules:** communicate posture, number, scale, and relationship before age, gender, ethnicity, occupation, or morality. Do not assign identity from silhouette alone.
- **Value build:** use four to six functional bands—void, structural dark, coloured shadow, luminous midtone, pale convergence, and optional deep cast shadow.
- **Edge build:** hard-enough core, soft illuminated perimeter, and irregular particulate gaps. A uniform outer glow is incorrect.

## Composition And Cropping

- **Focal point:** one silhouette, compact relational cluster, luminous center, or directional route.
- **Negative space:** maintain a continuous calm field, often in the upper or expected-action side. Darkness is an active compositional plane, not empty filler.
- **Safe text zones:** reserve a stable low-texture area. If the crop changes the local contrast, move the text or add an opaque carbon plate.
- **Aspect-ratio behavior:** design identity art in 3:4 first. Derive 1:1, 4:5, 16:9, and 9:16 crops from a documented recognition core and axis, not from automatic center crop.
- **Responsive crop rules:** crop atmosphere and secondary modules first; preserve the subject category and directional expectation. On wide screens, extend void or route; on narrow screens, reduce peripheral repetitions.
- **Sequence rhythm:** alternate intimate iconic plates with wider processional views. Do not use six nearly identical centered portraits in a row.

## Material And Lighting Cues

- **Surface finish:** matte, porous, dry, pigment-like, and light-absorbing.
- **Shadow behavior:** broad, deep, and directional; shadows may become compositional shapes. Avoid glossy contact shadows and floating-card elevation.
- **Reflection:** minimal. Replace mirror reflections with ground spill, haze, or coloured edge contamination.
- **Atmosphere:** dry particulate air, soft diffusion, and suspended light. It should clarify depth without becoming opaque fog.
- **Time of day:** nocturnal, pre-dawn, dusk, interior darkness, or an abstract light-starved stage. Midday scenes require deliberate inversion and must retain the dark structural field.
- **Light recipe:** one dominant thermal territory, one narrower opposing territory, one scarce pale intersection, and no broad neutral fill.

## Generated Asset Prompting

- **Positive visual facts:** tall portrait field; large active black void; one iconic silhouette or compact cluster; contour-first recognition; unequal ember-orange and mineral-cyan illumination; luminous ground, route, or rim; broad atmospheric depth; sparse staging; multiscale pigment-like grain; soft particulate edges; suspended narrative.
- **Negative constraints:** no named-artist imitation, photoreal face detail, glossy 3D render, generic orange-and-teal grading, sepia, distressed paper, vector outlines, anime linework, neon cyberpunk, broad white fog, multiple focal flares, text, watermark, fake film scratches, compulsory weapons, or novelty frontier decoration.
- **Reference-image roles:** use only licensed material and assign it a narrow role—composition, palette relationship, silhouette clarity, texture scale, or light behavior. Never ask a model to reproduce a complete scene or creator identity.
- **Variant variables:** subject category, count, governing axis, intimate versus processional scale, warm- or cool-dominant field, ground/route/light-pool anchor, optional contextual module, and crop ratio.
- **Failure checks:** subject illegible at thumbnail; dark core missing; orange/cyan equalized; smooth gradient replaces particulate collision; glow outlines every edge; faces over-rendered; props become clichés; multiple events compete; grain resembles JPEG damage; text or pseudo-watermark appears.

## Accessibility And Localization

- **Alt-text intent:** describe the content and purpose first—subject, action or relationship, setting, and function. Mention colour, grain, or mood only when they matter to the user’s task.
- **Cultural sensitivity:** review frontier, colonial, Indigenous, firearm, animal, land, and migration imagery with relevant cultural and subject-matter expertise. Do not infer affiliation or use sacred, traumatic, or stereotyped forms as atmosphere.
- **Text-in-image limits:** keep essential, translatable, searchable, selectable, and screen-readable text outside raster or generated imagery.
- **CVD/contrast concerns:** warm/cool atmosphere cannot carry unique information. Provide subject boundaries, labels, patterns, or structural separation that survive protan, deutan, tritan, greyscale, and high-contrast viewing.
- **Motion/media:** provide captions, transcripts, audio description where needed, controls for autoplay, and a reduced-motion version that preserves event and hierarchy.
- **Photosensitivity:** avoid flashing convergence light, rapid thermal alternation, high-frequency animated grain, and large pulsing fields.

## Translation Rules

1. Establish the black field and silhouette in greyscale.
2. Prove the one-event composition at thumbnail size.
3. Assign the dominant axis and depth family.
4. Add the larger thermal territory, then the narrower opposite field.
5. Create a small pale convergence point attached to the event.
6. Add atmosphere and grain while protecting recognition and support text.
7. Test the final crop, encoding, colour modes, accessibility alternatives, and cultural context.

## Anti-Patterns

- Equating the language with cowboy costumes, saloons, weapons, cacti, bison, or any other single prop family.
- Generic moody stock photography with a teal/orange LUT and noise overlay.
- Anonymous silhouettes used for real people or contexts requiring representation and accountability.
- Crops that retain atmosphere while cutting the subject’s only recognition cue.
- Photoreal faces pasted into low-detail bodies, over-described scenery, glossy light, or clean vector contours.
- Unlicensed source-pixel reuse, scene tracing, creator-name prompting, or claims of historical or cultural authenticity without review.
